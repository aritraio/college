# Short Answer Questions

**1. Differentiate between functional and non-functional requirements.**
**Answer:** Functional requirements define what the system must *do*—its features, calculations, data manipulation, and specific behaviors in response to inputs (e.g., "The system must generate a monthly sales report"). Non-functional requirements define *how* the system should perform those functions, imposing constraints on attributes like performance, security, usability, and reliability (e.g., "The report must generate in under 3 seconds").

**2. Explain the purpose of a Data Dictionary.**
**Answer:** A Data Dictionary acts as a central repository for metadata. Its purpose is to define and document all data elements, data structures, and data flows present within process models (like DFDs). It ensures consistency in terminology across the development team, eliminates redundancy, and provides clear, unambiguous definitions of data constraints and types.

**3. What are domain requirements? Provide an example.**
**Answer:** Domain requirements are derived from the application domain of the system rather than the direct requests of users. They reflect the fundamental characteristics, rules, or constraints of that specific industry or environment.
*Example:* In a medical records system, a domain requirement would be strict adherence to HIPAA compliance for data privacy; in a banking system, it might be a specific formula required by law for calculating compound interest.

**4. Briefly describe the importance of a Feasibility Study.**
**Answer:** A feasibility study is critical because it prevents the waste of resources on doomed projects. It assesses whether a project is viable from technical (can we build it?), economic (is it cost-effective?), and operational (will the organization use it?) standpoints. It serves as a go/no-go decision point before major development investments are made.

**5. How do scenarios differ from use-cases in requirement elicitation?**
**Answer:** Scenarios are informal, narrative descriptions of a specific sequence of events or a single instance of a user interacting with the system. They are concrete and often written in natural language. Use-cases are more formal, structured models that define all possible scenarios (including success paths and failure paths) for a specific goal an actor wants to achieve with the system.
