# Short Answer Questions

**1. Why is it a strategic failure to choose the machine learning algorithm before defining the business problem?**
**Answer:** Choosing an algorithm first is a "solution looking for a problem." Different business problems require different outputs (e.g., a simple binary decision vs. a continuous numerical forecast) and have varying constraints regarding latency and interpretability. Defining the business problem dictates the success metrics, which in turn dictate the appropriate algorithm.

**2. Outline the strategic value of executing a pilot project for a fraud detection system.**
**Answer:** A pilot project minimizes financial and operational risk. It allows the business to test the fraud model on a restricted subset of transactions to verify accuracy, measure false positive rates, and ensure the system integrates with existing workflows before committing massive resources to a full-scale rollout.

**3. How does machine learning fundamentally change the approach to IT issue management?**
**Answer:** Traditional IT management is reactive; teams respond to alerts after a system has failed. Machine learning enables a proactive approach by continuously analyzing server logs, network traffic, and hardware telemetry to detect subtle anomalies that precede a failure, allowing teams to intervene before downtime occurs.

**4. State three essential machine learning skills necessary for implementing business solutions.**
**Answer:**
1. **Data Wrangling:** The ability to clean and prepare raw business data.
2. **Model Selection/Evaluation:** Knowing how to choose the right algorithm and evaluate it using metrics that align with business goals.
3. **Domain Knowledge:** Understanding the specific business context (e.g., healthcare regulations or financial systems) to ensure practical applicability.

**5. Briefly describe the practical relationship between model training and prediction algorithms.**
**Answer:** Model training is the preparatory phase where an algorithm processes historical data to learn underlying patterns and define its mathematical parameters. Once training is complete and validated, the resulting model becomes the prediction algorithm, which is then deployed to process new, incoming data and generate actionable outputs.
